2. **The Level 3 mechanism gap (§V-C) is now substantially resolved, in a narrower form than originally posed, with the remainder precisely scoped.** Resolved: the original Theorem 3 was near-tautological and has been replaced with a code-verified, non-trivial sufficient condition (§VI). Also resolved: Level 3 now has an explicit, checkable physical-feasibility objective (direction (b) of the old open item) — select, among caller-supplied candidate routes, the first (after Level 1/2 regeneration) whose $m_{\mathrm{phys}} \ge m_{\mathrm{safe}}$ — matching what `plan_route` actually implements; §V-C and §VII were both corrected to stop describing this as invoking "the geometric-escape mechanism," which this codebase does not implement at all (a real inconsistency this pass found and fixed, not merely an unfilled gap). 3. **Theorem 4 (recursive feasibility) has now been attempted and is resolved in a two-part form, with the boundary between what is and is not certified measured rather than asserted.** Resolved: the terminal safe set $\mathcal{X}_f$ the original item asked for exists, is exactly characterized (§VI), has the recursive-feasibility property $x_k \in \mathcal{X}_f \Rightarrow x_{k+1} \in \mathcal{X}_f$ by an exact proof, and is decided by a scalar `local_planner.online_step` already computes on every Level-4 cycle. It is not vacuous on the §VIII platform — it admits 100/98.8/42.9/19.4/7.3% of sampled states at 0/2/5/8/12 kg payload — which is the condition the source planning document set for including the result at all, and 0 invariance violations were found across 6,708 successor tests. Also resolved, in the direction the item's second clause anticipated: the *executed* fallback is a different object from the certified reference, and Theorem 5 covers it separately via a Lyapunov argument, soundly but over a conservative low-braking-energy region. Still open, and now named precisely rather than left implicit: a robust invariance result tolerating arbitrary closed-loop tracking error, which is what would certify Level 4 without qualification at every state where it fires. The measured size of that gap (saturations in 0/36/57/85/94 of 150 executed rollouts from states in $\mathcal{X}_f$), and the tested candidate fix that narrows it to 0/0/2/4/15 without closing it, are in §VI's Proposition 6; full numbers and seeds in `code/experiments/theorem4_terminal_set.py`.

16. **The cross-paper margin-stacking question has now been checked directly against the pHRI/interaction-dynamics draft (`pHRI/arXiv/phri_ICRA.tex`), not merely flagged, and the answer is: partially reconciled, with one specific unresolved gap.** The two margins do not naively double-stack: that paper's applied-torque constraint (its eq. for $\tau_{\mathrm{base}} + J_v^\top F_{\mathrm{mpc}}$) is a hard box at the raw $\tau_{\max}$, not a second buffer subtracted from it, so this paper's $m_{\mathrm{safe}}$ and that paper's constraint are not simply additive over the same limit. But a real, specific gap remains: this paper's $\Delta\tau_i$ (§IV) is scoped to model mismatch and *prediction* error, not to the additional torque an active, disturbance-rejecting interaction controller (exactly what that paper implements) may deliberately spend to preserve contact tracking under a real-time disturbance the certificate's own prediction horizon did not anticipate — so Theorem 1's guarantee is not shown to survive composition with such a controller as-is. §XII states this precisely, including the concrete candidate fix (an explicit interface budget $\tau_{\max} - m_{\mathrm{safe}}$ handed to the interaction layer instead of the raw $\tau_{\max}$) as an open question, not an assumed-fine composition. Resolving it — analytically or empirically — is left to future work and would require touching both papers, not just this one.

19. **A real soundness bug was found in the route-level (and online) Level-2 reshape certificate re-check, fixed, and it invalidates §IX-H's second finding (item 12): the "reshape succeeds, $m_{\mathrm{phys}}=2.55$" claim.** `_search_reshape_whole_route` and `online_step`'s Level-2 branch each sample the environment force *once*, from the *nominal* trajectory's positions, before the reshape QP runs — then reused that same stale array for the post-hoc `cert.m_phys(Q_new, ...)` re-verification of the *reshaped* trajectory. Because the contact force used throughout §IX-H/Experiment 7 is position-dependent, and the reshaped path here dips deeper into the field than the nominal one (`ee_z=0.144` vs. `0.234`, contact plane at `z=0.55` — the paper's own text already noted the reshaped path's minimum height is "if anything, slightly lower" without recognizing this invalidated the force term in the feasibility check), the certificate check ran against a nominal-position force of 94.7N when the reshaped path's true force there is 121.7N. Independently recomputing the margin with forces resampled at the reshaped positions gives $m_{\mathrm{phys}}=-0.14$, not the reported $+2.55$. **Fixed** in `local_planner.py` (both call sites now resample forces at the QP's returned `Q_new` before the certificate check); `test_planner.py`'s corresponding test rewritten to assert the corrected (negative) outcome and guard against the false positive recurring. Confirmed no other reported result is affected: the flagship scenario (§IX-B) uses no `ee_force_fn` at all (a static payload/gravity deficit, position-independent in the relevant sense) and is unaffected; Experiment 7 (§IX-G) does use a position-dependent force alongside reshape and was rerun — its "reshape cannot resolve" conclusion (B3 still reroutes, Level 3) is unchanged. Separately, a broader 3000-trial sweep (both an adversarial and a realistic-magnitude regime) checked whether the reshape QP's *own* frozen-linearization search (which evaluates $M(q), h(q,\dot q)$ at the nominal state, not the optimization variables — item 2's flagged conservatism gap) could itself produce a false-positive *accepted* Level-2 success: zero true-torque counterexamples were found among solutions the real acceptance gate (`cert.m_phys`, which always uses the true nonlinear dynamics at the true returned state) actually accepts, in either regime — confirming the gate itself is sound; an earlier internal check that appeared to show a QP-linearization soundness gap had incorrectly treated "the QP solver reports optimal" as equivalent to "the planner accepts the result," which is not what any real call site does. **Net effect on the paper**: §IX-H's second finding should be corrected or removed — as far as currently demonstrated, no scenario in this benchmark shows route-level reshape genuinely succeeding under a position-dependent force field; every actually-verified adaptation success in this draft (flagship, Experiment 7) is via rerouting (Level 3), not reshaping. The first and third findings of §IX-H (reshape cannot resolve the two reroute-required scenarios; the whole-route QP's real-time cost) are unaffected. Full diagnostic code: `code/experiments/verify_reshape_acceptance_gate.py`, `code/experiments/reshape_linearization_gap.py`.
